# Management Intelligence v5
## AI Reasoning Model

**Status:** Draft for architectural review
**Session:** 011
**Scope:** Canonical reasoning framework for all Intelligence Domains

---

# 1. Purpose

The AI Reasoning Model defines how every Intelligence Domain transforms operational data into Management Decisions.

The purpose is not to replace management.

The purpose is to improve the consistency, quality, explainability, and effectiveness of management decision-making.

Every Intelligence Domain uses the same reasoning model.

Only the business knowledge changes.

---

# 2. Guiding Principles

Management Intelligence shall always be:

- Evidence Based
- Explainable
- Traceable
- Decision Focused
- Outcome Oriented
- Continuously Learning

Every recommendation must be explainable through evidence.

Every decision must remain accountable to human leadership.

# 4. Observation

An Observation is an objective statement about operational reality.

Examples:

- GP/RO increased $17.
- Technician efficiency declined 8%.
- Video creation reached 93%.
- Open ROs increased by 21.

Observations contain no interpretation.

---

# 5. Signal

Signals indicate meaningful change.

Examples:

- Positive Trend
- Negative Trend
- Capacity Constraint
- Workflow Delay
- Financial Risk
- Customer Risk
- Operational Opportunity

Signals answer:

"What deserves management attention?"

---

# 6. Insight

Insights explain likely causes.

Examples:

- Advisor presentation compliance declined.
- Dispatch bottleneck reduced technician utilization.
- Completed-status ROs delayed financial recognition.
- Capacity is sufficient, but workflow is constrained.

Insights answer:

"Why is this happening?"

---

# 7. Risk

Risk estimates probable future negative outcomes.

Examples:

- Budget Miss Risk
- CSI Risk
- Capacity Risk
- Retention Risk
- Margin Risk
- Employee Burnout Risk

Risk answers:

"What happens if nothing changes?"

---

# 8. Opportunity

Opportunity estimates potential improvement.

Examples:

- Additional GP Opportunity
- Hours Opportunity
- Capacity Opportunity
- Advisor Coaching Opportunity
- Technician Development Opportunity

Opportunity answers:

"What can improve?"

---

# 9. Recommendation Output

Recommendations should include:

- Problem Statement
- Supporting Evidence
- Confidence
- Expected Financial Impact
- Expected Operational Impact
- Expected Customer Impact
- Recommended Actions
- Risks
- Assumptions

Recommendations never make decisions.

---

# 10. Management Decision

Management Decisions belong to leaders.

The AI recommends.

Leadership decides.

Every Management Decision should capture:

- Decision
- Decision Owner
- Business Objective
- Expected Outcome
- Review Date
- Success Measurements

---

# 11. Execution

Execution converts decisions into operational behavior.

Execution includes:

- Tasks
- Coaching
- Meetings
- Training
- Process Changes
- Follow-up

Poor execution should never be confused with poor decision quality.

---

# 12. Validation

Validation compares:

Expected Results

versus

Actual Results

Validation evaluates:

- Decision Quality
- Execution Quality
- Assumptions
- Evidence

---

# 13. Organizational Learning

Validated decisions become organizational knowledge.

The platform continuously learns:

- What works
- What fails
- Under what conditions
- For which leaders
- In which stores
- Against which constraints

Future recommendations should improve because previous decisions are remembered.

---

# 14. Reasoning Patterns

Every Intelligence Domain should support these reasoning models.

## Constraint Analysis

What is preventing success?

## Trend Analysis

What is changing?

## Variance Analysis

What differs from expectation?

## Root Cause Analysis

Why?

## Leverage Analysis

Where will one improvement create the greatest impact?

## Comparative Analysis

Who performs differently?

## Pattern Recognition

Has this happened before?

## Decision Recall

How was this solved previously?

## Risk Forecasting

What happens if nothing changes?

## Opportunity Forecasting

What is the expected upside?

## Counterfactual Reasoning

What would likely happen under a different decision?

## Validation Reasoning

Did the decision accomplish the intended objective?

---

# 15. Explainability

Every recommendation should answer:

What happened?

Why?

What evidence supports it?

How confident is the conclusion?

What should management consider?

What assumptions exist?

What are the expected outcomes?

---

# 16. Philosophy

Management Intelligence exists to improve management thinking.

Its success is measured not by the number of recommendations it produces, but by the quality of the decisions it helps leaders make and the organizational learning it preserves for the future.
