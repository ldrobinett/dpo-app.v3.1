## Recommendation Engine

**Status:** Draft for architectural review
**Session:** 012
**Scope:** Canonical Recommendation Engine

---

# 1. Purpose

The Recommendation Engine transforms Intelligence Findings into prioritized management recommendations.

Its purpose is not to make management decisions.

Its purpose is to identify where leadership attention will create the greatest business impact.

Recommendations provide disciplined guidance supported by evidence.

Management remains responsible for every decision.

---

# 2. Mission

The Recommendation Engine answers one question:

**"What should management pay attention to next?"**

Recommendations should always prioritize:

- Business Impact
- Urgency
- Confidence
- Strategic Alignment
- Organizational Learning

---

# 3. Inputs

The Recommendation Engine consumes:

- Intelligence Findings
- Signals
- Risks
- Opportunities
- Business Measurements
- Objectives
- Constraints
- Organizational Learning
- Historical Management Decisions

---

# 4. Recommendation Lifecycle

```text
Observation
        ↓
Signal
        ↓
Insight
        ↓
Risk / Opportunity
        ↓
Recommendation
        ↓
Management Decision
```

Recommendations exist only to support Management Decisions.

# 7. Prioritization Model

Recommendations should be ranked using multiple dimensions.

## Business Impact

Expected operational improvement.

## Financial Impact

Estimated contribution to revenue, gross profit, or expense.

## Customer Impact

Expected effect on customer experience.

## Urgency

Time sensitivity.

## Confidence

Strength of supporting evidence.

## Strategic Alignment

Support for current business objectives.

# 8. Recommendation Portfolio

The Recommendation Portfolio organizes recommendations into a manageable leadership workload.

Its purpose is not to display every recommendation.

Its purpose is to help leaders focus on the highest-value decisions.

Leadership attention is a finite organizational resource.

The Recommendation Engine should maximize the return on that resource.

---

## Portfolio Categories

### Critical Now

Immediate action required.

Characteristics:

- High financial impact
- High customer impact
- High operational risk
- High confidence
- Time sensitive

Examples:

- Technician shortage affecting today's production
- Large completed-status Repair Orders delaying month-end close
- Significant CSI failure requiring immediate intervention

---

### Operational Priorities

Execution opportunities expected to improve near-term performance.

Examples:

- Improve advisor video presentation
- Increase same-day conversion
- Reduce open Repair Orders
- Improve technician dispatch efficiency

These recommendations typically drive measurable improvement within days or weeks.

---

### Strategic Initiatives

Long-term management improvements.

Examples:

- Technician development programs
- Leadership coaching
- Capacity planning
- Staffing strategy
- Process redesign
- Organizational restructuring

These recommendations support sustainable competitive advantage.

---

### Monitor

Conditions requiring observation rather than immediate action.

Examples:

- Emerging performance trends
- Minor budget variance
- Capacity nearing threshold
- Early customer satisfaction decline

Monitor recommendations should automatically escalate if risk increases.

---

### Resolved

Recommendations that have completed the full Management Decision lifecycle.

Resolved recommendations include:

- Decision
- Execution
- Validation
- Business Outcome
- Organizational Learning

Resolved recommendations become part of the enterprise knowledge base.

---

# Portfolio Management

The Recommendation Portfolio should remain intentionally small.

Recommendations should compete for leadership attention.

The Recommendation Engine should:

- Consolidate similar recommendations
- Eliminate duplicate recommendations
- Retire obsolete recommendations
- Escalate increasing risks
- De-escalate resolved conditions

The objective is not maximum visibility.

The objective is maximum management effectiveness.

---

# Portfolio Health

Portfolio effectiveness may be measured using:

- Active Recommendations
- Critical Recommendations
- Average Recommendation Age
- Recommendation Acceptance Rate
- Recommendation Completion Rate
- Recommendation Validation Rate
- Recommendation Success Rate
- Organizational Learning Contribution

A healthy portfolio focuses leadership attention on the few decisions most likely to improve organizational performance.

# 8. Recommendation Quality

A high-quality recommendation is:

- Evidence Based
- Explainable
- Actionable
- Measurable
- Relevant
- Timely
- Traceable

Recommendations should never exist without supporting evidence.

---

# 9. Recommendation Fatigue

The Recommendation Engine should actively reduce unnecessary recommendations.

Recommendations should be:

- Consolidated
- Prioritized
- De-duplicated
- Context Aware

The objective is clarity.

Not volume.

---

# 10. Relationship to Management Decisions

Recommendations never become actions directly.

Every Recommendation must pass through Management Decision.

Leadership may:

- Accept
- Modify
- Defer
- Reject

The Recommendation Engine records the outcome to improve future recommendations.

---

# 11. Organizational Learning

Every Recommendation should eventually answer:

Was it accepted?

Was it executed?

Did it improve performance?

Was the reasoning correct?

Future recommendations improve through validated historical evidence.

---

# 12. Design Principles

The Recommendation Engine exists to improve management focus.

It should reduce cognitive overload rather than increase it.

The quality of a recommendation is measured by the quality of the management decisions it supports, not by the number of recommendations it generates.
