# Management Intelligence™ Knowledge Model

Version: 1.0
Status: Foundational Architecture
Date: July 2026

---

# Purpose

Management Intelligence™ is not a reporting engine.

It is a decision framework designed to transform operational data into managerial judgment.

This document defines the knowledge architecture that supports every future Management Intelligence capability.

Software implementation is secondary.

The knowledge model is the foundation.

---

# Core Philosophy

Management Intelligence™ exists to transform operational data into managerial judgment.

The objective is not simply to report what happened.

The objective is to recommend what should happen next.

Every recommendation must be:

• Explainable

• Measurable

• Financially justifiable

• Operationally actionable

• Capable of learning from future outcomes

---

# Knowledge Hierarchy

Management Intelligence™ is built from seven layers of knowledge.

```
Measurements
      ↓
Derived Metrics
      ↓
Health Metrics
      ↓
Operational Constraints
      ↓
Risk Assessments
      ↓
Management Decisions
      ↓
Learning & Continuous Improvement
```

Each layer builds upon the previous layer.

No recommendation should bypass the hierarchy.

---

# Layer 1 — Measurements

Measurements are objective facts.

Measurements contain no interpretation.

Examples include:

• Flat Rate Hours (FRH)

• Gross Profit

• Labor Gross

• Parts Gross

• Effective Labor Rate (ELR)

• Hours Sold

• Repair Orders

• Appointments

• Technician Hours

• Daily Production Objective (Raw)

• Videos Created

• Videos Viewed

• Tasks Completed

• Warranty to CP Conversions

• Tire Sales

• Brake Sales

• Battery Sales

• Alignment Sales

• CSI

• Expense

• Absorption

Measurements answer:

"What exists?"

---

# Layer 2 — Derived Metrics

Derived metrics are mathematical relationships created from measurements.

Examples include:

• Pace

• Expected Gross

• Gross Deficit

• Production Gap

• Capacity Gap

• Recovery Hours

• Average Hours per RO

• Appointment Need

• Forecast Utilization

• WIP Capacity

• Daily Production Target

• Month-End Projection

Derived metrics answer:

"What does the data mean?"

---

# Layer 3 — Organizational Health Metrics

Health metrics measure the long-term capability of the organization.

Unlike measurements, they describe organizational strength.

Examples include:

## Daily Production Objective (DPO)

Measures expected technician production.

Purpose:

Production planning.

---

## Utilization Variance Index (UVI)

Measures production concentration.

Purpose:

Organizational dependency risk.

Questions answered:

• How dependent are we on our top producers?

• What happens if one technician is unavailable?

• Is production evenly distributed?

---

## Technician Sustainability Index (TSI)

Measures future production sustainability.

Purpose:

Organizational development.

Questions answered:

• Are technicians progressing?

• Is mentoring occurring?

• Is the workforce becoming stronger?

---

Future Health Metrics may include:

• Advisor Capacity Index

• Service Capacity Index

• Leadership Effectiveness Index

• Organizational Sustainability Score

Health metrics answer:

"How healthy is the operation?"

---

# Layer 4 — Operational Constraints

Constraints are conditions that limit production.

Constraints prevent organizational throughput.

Examples include:

Workflow Constraints

• Dispatch

• Inspection

• Approval

• Parts

• Warranty

• Ready Status

Operational Constraints

• Technician Capacity

• Appointment Load

• Loaner Availability

• Staffing

• Tool Availability

Execution Constraints

• Videos

• Tasks

• Welcome Pack Completion

• Customer Contact

Constraints answer:

"What is preventing success?"

---

# Layer 5 — Risk Assessments

Risk combines measurements, health metrics, and constraints.

Risk predicts future operational outcomes.

Examples include:

Production Risk

Customer Experience Risk

Financial Risk

Capacity Risk

Technician Concentration Risk

Technician Sustainability Risk

Leadership Risk

Execution Risk

Risk answers:

"What happens if we do nothing?"

---

# Layer 6 — Management Decisions

Decision objects transform knowledge into action.

Every decision should produce:

Primary Issue

Operational Blocker

Priority Level

Recommended Actions

Expected Financial Impact

Expected Customer Impact

Expected Operational Improvement

Recommended Tool

Confidence Level

Decision objects answer:

"What should the manager do next?"

---

# Layer 7 — Learning & Continuous Improvement

Learning separates Management Intelligence™ from static reporting.

Every recommendation should eventually create feedback.

Learning includes:

Recommendation History

Action Completion

Financial Outcome

Operational Outcome

CSI Outcome

Recommendation Accuracy

Confidence Adjustment

Recommendation Weighting

Learning answers:

"What worked?"

---

# Decision Framework

Every Management Intelligence™ recommendation should follow the same progression.

```
Measure

↓

Interpret

↓

Assess Organizational Health

↓

Identify Constraints

↓

Evaluate Risk

↓

Recommend Action

↓

Measure Outcome

↓

Learn
```

No recommendation should skip a step.

---

# Design Principles

Every Management Intelligence™ feature should satisfy the following questions.

1. What measurements support this?

2. What derived metrics are required?

3. Which health metrics are involved?

4. What constraint is limiting performance?

5. What risks exist?

6. What decision should be made?

7. How will the outcome be measured?

8. How will the system improve from the result?

---

# Relationship to ProdTracker

ProdTracker is the software implementation.

Management Intelligence™ is the decision framework.

The software exists to operationalize the Management Intelligence™ knowledge model.

The knowledge model remains independent of any programming language or technology.

---

# Guiding Principle

Management Intelligence™ never tells a manager what happened.

Management Intelligence™ tells a manager:

• What matters most.

• Why it matters.

• What to do next.

• What result is expected.

• Whether the recommendation improved the operation.

---

# Future Vision

The long-term objective is to create a Management Intelligence™ platform that allows a developing manager to consistently make decisions approaching the quality, consistency, and reasoning of an experienced operational leader.

The platform does not replace leadership.

It preserves, organizes, and scales managerial judgment.
