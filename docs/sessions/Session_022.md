2. Canonical Domain Purpose

Forecasting, Planning & Scenario Intelligence uses current operating evidence, historical behavior, known commitments, available capacity, emerging conditions, and intervention effectiveness to estimate likely future outcomes, identify material risks and opportunities to those outcomes, and help management evaluate alternative actions before results become fixed.

Forecasting is not target-setting. Planning is not merely dividing a financial gap by remaining days. Scenario Intelligence is not prediction disguised as arithmetic.

3. Future-State Integrity

MI distinguishes five future-state concepts:

Current Result: what has actually happened.

Operating Trajectory: where current behavior appears to be taking the operation if material conditions remain substantially unchanged.

Forecast Outcome: MI's evidence-supported estimate or range of a future result.

Plan: what management intends to change through selected pathways and interventions.

Scenario: what could occur under explicit alternative assumptions.

Future-State Integrity Principle: Actual results, trajectory, forecast, plan, and scenario are distinct concepts and must never be presented interchangeably.

4. Operating Trajectory

Operating Trajectory: the evidence-supported direction and rate at which an operating or economic result is developing if material conditions remain substantially unchanged.

Trajectory is not the same as a forecast. It describes the direction implied by current behavior.

5. Forecast Outcome

Forecast Outcome: the evidence-supported estimate or range of a future result based on current trajectory, known future conditions, historical behavior, and explicit assumptions.

MI should avoid false precision. A forecast range with clear confidence and uncertainty drivers is generally more decision-useful than an unjustifiably precise number.

6. Forecast Confidence

Forecast Confidence: MI's assessment of how strongly available evidence supports a forecast based on evidence quality, historical stability, known uncertainty, changing conditions, assumption dependency, and forecast horizon.

External presentation may use understandable levels such as High, Moderate, and Low while the internal model may retain more detailed scoring.

7. Forecast Validity

Forecast Validity: whether the assumptions and operating conditions supporting an existing forecast remain sufficiently intact for the forecast to remain decision-useful.

A material disruption may invalidate a prior forecast and require recalibration.

8. Forecast Calibration and Bias

Forecast Calibration: the continuous comparison of prior forecasts against actual outcomes to determine whether MI's assumptions, weighting, confidence, or models require adjustment.

Forecast Bias: a persistent tendency for forecast outcomes to systematically exceed or fall below actual results.

Calibration may occur by metric, store, season, horizon, condition, or other supported context.

9. Leading Conditions, Early Warning, and Emerging Opportunity

Leading Condition: an observable operating condition whose change is expected to precede and materially influence a later operating or economic outcome.

Lagging Outcome: a result reflecting the accumulated effect of earlier operating conditions and decisions.

Early Warning Condition: a leading condition whose trajectory indicates material future risk before the associated lagging outcome has materially deteriorated.

Emerging Opportunity: a leading condition indicating future performance may exceed current expectations if management preserves or appropriately acts upon the developing condition.

10. Operational Foresight

Operational Foresight: the use of current evidence to identify likely near-term operating conditions early enough for management or employees to intervene before the condition becomes an outcome.

Operational Foresight allows the same future-state model to support immediate ASM/SM decisions without requiring a separate forecasting architecture.

11. Root-Cause Compression

The previously considered numerical Management Focus Limit is rejected.

Root-Cause Compression: MI continues reasoning upstream across related evidence until it identifies the smallest defensible set of root conditions capable of explaining and materially influencing the largest set of relevant downstream conditions.

Root-Cause Compression does not force artificial simplicity. If evidence supports several independent root causes, MI preserves them.

Root-Cause Compression Principle: MI seeks the smallest defensible set of root conditions, not the smallest imaginable set.

12. Causal Confidence

Causal Confidence: the degree to which available evidence supports MI's conclusion that one condition materially contributes to another observed condition.

MI should not equate correlation or timing with proven causation.

13. Planning Intelligence

Planning Intelligence identifies the evidence-supported changes in operating conditions most likely to improve the trajectory toward a desired outcome and helps management select, sequence, and evaluate those changes.

Planning focuses on conditions and management pathways rather than merely restating desired outcomes.

14. Planning Gap

Planning Gap: the difference between a desired outcome and the outcome currently supported by operating trajectory and identified opportunity.

MI does not manufacture unsupported opportunity merely to make the desired outcome appear achievable.

15. Management Pathway

Management Pathway: an evidence-supported sequence of operating changes through which management can reasonably influence a desired outcome.

A pathway preserves causal reasoning from management action through operating condition to downstream result.

16. Pathway Confidence

Pathway Confidence: the degree to which available evidence supports the expected relationship between a proposed operating change and the desired outcome.

17. Planning Priority

Planning Priority: the relative management importance of an evidence-supported pathway based on expected impact, urgency, controllability, confidence, dependencies, and current operating context.

MI should prioritize through Root-Cause Compression rather than imposing an arbitrary numerical focus limit.

18. Desired Outcome, Operating Condition, and Management Action

Desired Outcome: the result management wants to influence.

Operating Condition: the current state affecting that result.

Management Action: a specific behavior or intervention intended to change the operating condition.

MI must not mistake an outcome statement such as “improve CSI” or “increase tire penetration” for a management action.

19. Intervention Fit

Intervention Fit: the degree to which a proposed management intervention addresses the evidence-supported root condition responsible for the desired outcome.

Potential external classifications may include Strong, Partial, Weak, and Unsupported.

MI may challenge a proposed action when evidence indicates the action addresses a downstream symptom rather than the root condition.

20. Management Authority and Override

Management Authority Principle: MI advises, challenges, models, and learns, but authorized human management retains decision authority.

A manager may select an intervention different from MI's recommendation. MI records the decision, captures relevant context, observes execution and results, and learns from the outcome.

Manager-provided context becomes evidence subject to normal evidence confidence and governance. It is not unquestioned truth.

21. Planning Dependency and Action Sequence

Planning Dependency: a condition that must exist or be sufficiently resolved before another management pathway can reasonably produce its intended effect.

Action Sequence: the evidence-supported ordering of dependent management interventions intended to improve the probability that each intervention can produce its desired effect.

Examples include capacity before incremental demand, inspection before presentation, and capability before specialized work assignment.

22. Plan Sufficiency and Feasibility

Plan Sufficiency: the degree to which selected management pathways reasonably address the magnitude and nature of the identified Planning Gap.

Plan Feasibility: the degree to which a management plan can reasonably be executed given available demand, capacity, capability, time, dependencies, and other operating constraints.

A plan may be mathematically sufficient but operationally infeasible.

23. Dynamic Planning

Dynamic Planning: the continuous reassessment of a management plan as material operating evidence changes during the planning horizon.

The Digital FOD should recognize when the problem changes rather than mechanically continuing an obsolete morning, weekly, or monthly plan.

24. No Artificial Action Plan

No Artificial Action Plan Principle: MI must not generate management actions merely because a metric is below expectation. Recommended actions must trace to an evidence-supported operating condition and a plausible Management Pathway.

25. Scenario Intelligence

Scenario: an evidence-supported representation of a possible future operating state created by changing one or more explicit assumptions, conditions, or management decisions.

Scenario Integrity Principle: MI clearly distinguishes modeled scenarios from forecasts and actual operating trajectory. A Scenario describes what could occur under explicit assumptions; it is not a prediction that those assumptions will occur.

26. Scenario Assumption, Confidence, and Validity

Scenario Assumption: an explicit condition treated as true for purposes of evaluating a modeled future state.

Scenario Confidence: the degree to which available evidence supports the modeled relationships and assumptions within a Scenario.

Scenario Validity: whether the evidence and assumptions underlying a previously modeled Scenario remain sufficiently current for that Scenario to remain decision-useful.

Materially stale scenarios require recalculation before use.

27. Scenario Constraints, Trade-Offs, and Second-Order Effects

Scenario Constraint: an operating condition limiting the degree to which a modeled Scenario can produce its expected result.

Scenario Trade-Off: a material positive or negative consequence expected to arise from a modeled management decision in addition to its primary intended outcome.

Second-Order Effect: a downstream operating or economic consequence caused indirectly by the initial change introduced in a Scenario.

MI should avoid optimizing a single KPI while ignoring material downstream consequences.

28. Scenario Comparison and Recommendation

Scenario alternatives may be compared using evidence-supported dimensions such as:

expected impact;

confidence;

time to effect;

required investment;

dependencies;

risk;

second-order effects;

horizon trade-offs.

A Scenario Recommendation identifies the alternative MI believes has the strongest evidence-supported management case. The recommendation must retain evidence and reasoning lineage.

29. Scenario Calibration

Scenario Calibration: the comparison of modeled Scenario outcomes with actual results when the modeled management change is subsequently implemented.

Scenario Calibration is distinct from Forecast Calibration and contributes to MI Effectiveness and Operational Wisdom.

30. Management Pattern

Management Pattern: a recurring evidence-supported relationship between operating conditions, management interventions, and observed outcomes.

Validated patterns may improve future Pathway Confidence, Scenario Confidence, and Digital FOD guidance subject to data governance and tenant boundaries.

31. Management Horizon

Management Horizon: the period over which an operating condition, forecast, plan, scenario, or management intervention is intended to remain relevant.

Canonical semantic classes may include:

Immediate

Near-Term

Intermediate

Longer-Term

Exact day boundaries are not globally hard-coded.

32. Horizon Trade-Off

Horizon Trade-Off: a condition in which an action beneficial within one management horizon creates material adverse consequences in another horizon.

MI surfaces material short-term versus longer-term conflicts rather than silently optimizing one horizon.

Horizon Integrity Principle: MI evaluates management actions across relevant time horizons and surfaces material conflicts between short-term benefit and longer-term operating consequence.

33. One Future-State Model

MI should not create separate contradictory futures for ASM, SM, FOD, GM, or DWP.

One Future-State Model Principle: MI maintains a coherent future-state model and varies resolution according to role, responsibility, horizon, materiality, entitlement, and evidence rather than creating independent versions of the future for each management level.

Typical resolution:

ASM: immediate Operational Foresight.

SM / PM: daily and monthly operating planning.

FOD: multi-condition and multi-store planning.

GM: operating, investment, and longer-range scenario intelligence.

DWP: leadership-level future-state risk, opportunity, and intervention intelligence.

34. Seasonal Pattern and Pattern Relevance

Seasonal Pattern: a recurring time-dependent variation in operating behavior supported by sufficient historical evidence.

Pattern Relevance: the degree to which a historically observed pattern remains supported by current operating evidence.

Historical evidence does not become permanent truth. MI continuously evaluates whether a prior pattern remains decision-useful.

35. Evidence Maturity

Evidence Maturity: the degree to which sufficient historical and current evidence exists to support reliable future-state reasoning for a particular condition, metric, store, or model.

A new tenant/store may receive useful future-state reasoning with lower confidence while store-specific evidence matures.

36. Structural Change Event

Structural Change Event: a material change in operating structure, leadership, process, capacity, policy, environment, or business model that may reduce the predictive relevance of prior historical behavior.

MI may temporarily reduce forecast confidence and increase the weighting of current evidence after a supported structural change.

37. External Condition

External Condition: a sufficiently supported condition originating outside the dealership operation that may materially influence future operating performance.

Examples may include weather, regional emergencies, OEM campaigns, stop-sales, strikes, regulatory changes, or major local events.

External conditions retain provenance, confidence, and actual-event follow-up for calibration.

38. Future Mix

Future Mix: the expected composition of future demand, work, customer, labor, parts, or economic activity where differences in composition materially affect the forecast.

MI must not assume equal economic or operating value from equal unit volume.

39. Management Intent

Management Intent: a management-declared future action or desired operating change that may inform planning but does not become forecast evidence until supported by execution or other reliable evidence.

A scheduled intervention may influence conditional planning, but forecast effects should update as execution and effectiveness become evidenced.

40. Assumption Provenance

Assumption Provenance: the traceable source, author or derivation, supporting evidence, confidence, effective period, and change history of a material forecast or scenario assumption.

Assumption Provenance protects future-state reasoning from silent forecast manipulation or unsupported optimism/pessimism.

41. Uncertainty Driver

Uncertainty Driver: a condition whose unresolved state materially increases the range or reduces the confidence of a future-state estimate.

MI should explain material uncertainty rather than merely display a wide forecast range.

42. Unexplained Forecast Error

Unexplained Forecast Error: the portion of difference between forecast and actual outcome not sufficiently explained by known assumptions, evidence changes, identified disruptions, or supported causal conditions.

MI records unexplained error rather than inventing post-hoc causation.

43. Objective Conflict

Objective Conflict: a condition in which actions supporting one desired outcome materially impair another governed or material outcome.

MI surfaces the trade-off and preserves human management authority over the decision.

44. Legitimate Outcome Protection

Legitimate Outcome Principle: No forecast, plan, scenario, target, budget, or financial objective may convert unsupported customer work, unnecessary activity, unsafe behavior, deceptive practice, or otherwise inappropriate conduct into legitimate opportunity.

Financial objectives do not redefine legitimate customer need.
