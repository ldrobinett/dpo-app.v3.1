1. Session Outcome

Domain 9 is frozen.

Operating Activity → Economic Event → Accounting Recognition → Canonical Economic Classification → Purpose → Controllability → Root Operating Cause → Economic Consequence → Absorption Impact → Materiality → Digital FOD Guidance → Management Intervention → Observed Effect → MI Learning

Domain 9 does not replace accounting. It interprets accounting and operating evidence so dealership leaders can understand what is driving economic performance, what can reasonably be influenced, and what management action deserves attention.

2. Domain 9 Boundary

Operating Control, Economic Integrity & Absorption Intelligence determines how effectively the operation converts available capacity and generated economic value into retained contribution and dealership expense absorption. It identifies material economic leakage, distinguishes legitimate operating investment from waste, explains financial outcomes through their operating causes, and guides management toward actions most likely to improve sustainable economic performance.

Accounting reports what happened financially.

The Digital FOD uses MI V5 to determine what operating condition created the result, whether management can influence it, and what action deserves attention.

3. Economic Event

Economic Event: a business occurrence that creates, transfers, preserves, reduces, invests, or consumes economic value within the operation.

Examples include:

labor or parts sales;

discounts or concessions;

policy/goodwill;

technician unapplied time;

sublet;

purchase incentives/rebates;

inventory write-offs;

equipment acquisition;

training/capability investment.

The Economic Event is distinct from the GL account or statement line in which accounting recognizes it.

4. Accounting Recognition

Accounting Recognition: the manner and period in which an Economic Event is recognized within the tenant's accounting and financial reporting structure.

A large equipment acquisition may be capitalized and depreciated according to tenant policy, expensed in the current period, or receive mixed treatment across equipment, installation, software, training, facility modification, or other components.

Accounting Recognition Principle: MI preserves tenant accounting treatment while independently understanding the underlying operating Economic Event.

MI does not infer economic meaning solely from accounting timing or statement placement.

5. Canonical Expense Structure

MI maintains an industry-neutral canonical economic taxonomy. Tenant financial accounts map into it.

Direct / Variable Operating Expense

Costs materially associated with performing work.

Personnel Expense

Management, advisor, technician where applicable, parts, support personnel, payroll-related expense, benefits, bonuses, overtime, and related workforce cost.

Selling / Customer Acquisition & Retention Expense

Marketing, acquisition, retention, customer communication, and similar selling costs.

Semi-Fixed Operating Expense

Tools, uniforms, software, shop-equipment support, supplies, training, waste handling, service schedulers, and similar operating infrastructure.

Fixed / Facility Expense

Occupancy, depreciation, insurance, facility obligations, and other longer-term structural expense.

Allocated / Shared Expense

Corporate, regional, administrative, IT, HR, centralized services, and other costs allocated into the operation.

Capital / Strategic Investment

Economic events creating longer-lived capability, capacity, or strategic value. Accounting treatment remains tenant governed.

Economic Benefit / Recovery

Rebates, incentives, recoveries, reimbursements, and similar positive economic events.

8. Economic Leakage

Economic Leakage: economic value that was created, available, or reasonably expected but was not retained because of an operating decision, execution gap, process failure, quality condition, control deficiency, or preventable capacity loss.

Canonical leakage families include:

Labor Capture Leakage

Unapplied labor, unbilled legitimate labor, poor labor-price realization, preventable overtime, and productive-capacity loss.

Parts Capture Leakage

Pricing overrides, margin erosion, unrecovered freight, unsupported discounting, preventable missed incentives, and supported inventory loss.

Transaction Leakage

Unsupported discounts, missed charges, write-offs, overrides, posting problems, and uncollected legitimate customer responsibility.

Quality Leakage

Comebacks, repeat repairs, vehicle damage, rework, and preventable policy/goodwill.

Process Leakage

Avoidable sublet, unnecessary outside services, and process-generated cost.

Capacity Leakage

Available productive capability not converted into useful output.

9. Unapplied Labor

Unapplied Labor: paid technician labor capacity that was not applied to revenue-producing or otherwise recognized productive work during the measurement period.

MI evaluates cause before classifying the economic condition:

demand deficiency;

dispatch/workflow;

parts constraint;

capability mismatch;

training;

ramp/development;

process delay;

other supported condition.

Training and legitimate ramp/development are not automatically leakage.

Unapplied Labor Economic Consequence: the supported direct labor cost and/or foregone productive value associated with unapplied technician capacity. MI preserves lineage so alternative analytical views do not double count the same loss.

10. Price Realization

Price Realization: the degree to which governed or intended selling value is captured in the completed transaction after discounts, concessions, overrides, and adjustments.

Price Realization may apply to labor, parts, sublet, fees, and other legitimate charges.

11. Policy / Goodwill Intelligence

Policy/goodwill is not automatically leakage.

MI may distinguish:

Quality Correction

Vehicle Damage

Customer Value Investment

OEM / Third-Party Recoverable

Manager Discretion

Unsupported / Unresolved

Where policy expense is the downstream consequence of another operating condition, corrective ownership remains with the root domain.

12. Absorption Intelligence

Fixed Operations Absorption: the degree to which gross profit generated by fixed operations covers the dealership operating expense base defined by the tenant's governed absorption policy.

The tenant governs the numerator, denominator, exclusions, allocations, accounting treatment, and other policy choices used for its reported calculation.

Reported Absorption

The tenant's governed absorption result.

Absorption Integrity Principle: MI may analyze, explain, decompose, forecast, and model Reported Absorption, but it must never silently alter the tenant's governed calculation. Scenario or modeled views remain explicitly distinct from Reported Absorption.

Absorption Driver Analysis

MI decomposes movement in Reported Absorption into supported drivers such as:

gross contribution;

expense;

leakage;

investment;

structural conditions;

accounting events.

It then traces those drivers to operating causes.

Evidence-Supported Absorption Opportunity

The improvement in Reported Absorption reasonably supported by currently identified gross, expense, leakage, capacity, and operating opportunities.

MI may show:

Current Reported Absorption

Identified Recoverable Opportunity

Evidence-Supported Potential

Gap to a tenant-selected scenario such as 90% or 100%

MI does not invent sufficient opportunity merely to reach a target.

13. Recurrence Classification

Material Economic Events may be classified as:

Non-recurring does not mean excluded from reported results. MI explains the event rather than cosmetically removing it.

14. Economic Materiality

Economic Materiality: the significance of an economic condition based on magnitude, persistence, trajectory, controllability, recurrence, operating consequence, absorption consequence, confidence, and relevance to management responsibility.

Relevant & Valuable Display remains the governing presentation principle. MI should not create management noise from immaterial financial variances.

15. Economic Lineage

Economic Lineage Principle: Every quantified economic consequence retains lineage to its originating Economic Event so MI may analyze the same condition through multiple intelligence dimensions without double-counting financial impact.

A single $10,000 unapplied-labor event may be relevant to Personnel Expense, Capacity Leakage, workflow, and absorption, but remains one $10,000 economic event.

16. Cross-Domain Economic Consequence

Domain 9 serves as an economic-consequence layer across MI without taking ownership away from the root operating domain.

Examples:

opportunity-discovery deficiency → missed gross opportunity;

diagnostic capability constraint → sublet or productive-capacity consequence;

quality/process failure → policy or rework consequence.

Economic Consequence Principle: Domain 9 quantifies and interprets economic consequences across MI while corrective ownership remains with the domain containing the root operating cause.

17. Tenant Financial Mapping Architecture

Canonical mapping chain:

Tenant Financial Source → Tenant Account → Tenant Account Meaning → Canonical Economic Classification → Purpose → Controllability → Operating Relationship → Absorption Treatment

MI owns canonical economic meaning.

The tenant owns its accounting implementation and terminology.

The same tenant account name may map differently for different tenants. A tenant account may also contain multiple economic meanings that require transaction-level refinement.

Mapping States

Confirmed Mapping

Inferred Mapping

Unresolved Mapping

MI should infer as much as reliable evidence permits and request human validation only for exceptions.

18. Financial Mapping Drift

Financial Mapping Drift: a material change in account usage, transaction composition, posting behavior, or financial classification that may invalidate or weaken an existing canonical mapping.

MI surfaces material drift for review rather than silently continuing an obsolete mapping.

19. Historical Financial Integrity

Financial mappings and tenant economic policies are effective-dated and versioned.

Historical Integrity Principle: Changes in financial mapping, account meaning, or accounting treatment must never silently rewrite historical economic interpretation.

20. Tenant Economic Policy

Tenant Economic Policy: the governed tenant configuration defining relevant financial and economic treatment used by MI.

Potential components include:

absorption definition;

account mappings;

expense classifications;

capitalization policy;

depreciation treatment;

policy/goodwill treatment;

purchase incentives/rebates;

pricing expectations;

sublet expectations;

discount authority;

allocation treatment;

materiality preferences.

MI guides configuration and minimizes manual setup wherever evidence permits.

21. Capability Entitlement Architecture — Platform Level

Domain 9 exposed a platform-wide requirement:

Canonical MI V5 → Product Capability → Tenant Entitlement → Role Permission → Evidence Availability → Relevant & Valuable Display

These states remain separate.

Capability

A defined intelligence function MI knows how to perform.

Capability Entitlement

The governed right of a tenant to access and use a defined MI capability based on purchased product, subscription, license, or contracted configuration.

Role Permission

The governed right of an authorized user or role to access a tenant-entitled capability and its permitted level of detail.

Evidence Availability

Whether sufficient reliable evidence exists for the entitled capability to operate.

A capability may be Entitled but Evidence Insufficient. That is not the same as Not Entitled.

Capability Dependency

A governed relationship identifying another capability or evidence source required for a capability to function correctly.

Tenant Capability Manifest

The source-of-truth configuration identifying purchased capabilities, enabled capabilities, effective dates, dependencies, evidence status, role permissions, and tenant-specific extensions.

Commercial packaging remains separate from canonical MI architecture.

22. Capability Entitlement Principles

Entitlement Principle: MI capabilities are independently governable by tenant entitlement based on purchased product, subscription, license, or contracted configuration.

Permission Principle: Tenant entitlement determines whether the organization has access to a capability; role permission determines which authorized users may access its intelligence and level of detail.

Evidence Independence Principle: Entitlement status, permission status, and evidence sufficiency are separate states and must not be conflated.

Commercial Neutrality Principle: Canonical MI V5 capabilities are built independently from commercial packaging so capabilities can later be bundled, licensed, enabled, disabled, or extended without redesigning the intelligence architecture.

23. DWP / ProdTracker / MI V5 / Digital FOD Architecture — Platform Level

The platform relationship is:

DWP Management Methodology / Operating Knowledge → Management Intelligence™ V5 → Digital FOD → Dealership Leadership Decision Support → Management Action → Operating Evidence & Results → MI Learning / Effectiveness Evaluation

ProdTracker™

The management decision platform through which capabilities are delivered.

Management Intelligence™ V5

The canonical evidence, reasoning, learning, and decision-support engine.

Digital FOD

The persistent management capability powered by MI V5 that helps dealership leaders recognize material conditions, make better decisions, execute appropriate actions, and determine whether those actions worked.

DWP

Provides and governs the management methodology, operating intelligence, product architecture, and continuous evolution behind the Digital FOD.

The Digital FOD does not replace dealership management. It makes good fixed-operations management continuously available.

24. Persistent Management Assistance

Persistent Management Assistance Principle: The Digital FOD extends sound fixed-operations management between direct leadership interactions by continuously observing evidence, identifying material conditions, guiding responsible management, evaluating intervention effectiveness, and escalating when human leadership attention becomes relevant.

The human FOD retains judgment, authority, coaching, relationships, and intervention.

The Digital FOD provides persistence, evidence, reasoning, follow-through, and learning.

25. Digital FOD Continuity Under Entitlement

A tenant that has operational intelligence but has not purchased Economic Intelligence may still receive competent operational guidance.

Example:

Productive technician capacity is underutilized. Review available work and dispatch.

It does not receive non-entitled economic interpretation such as quantified personnel leakage, absorption impact, or premium financial analysis.

Digital FOD Continuity Principle: The Digital FOD remains competent using the tenant's entitled capabilities without exposing calculations, interpretations, or outputs belonging to capabilities the tenant has not purchased.

26. Management Altitude

Management Altitude Principle: MI presents the condition, evidence, action, and detail appropriate to the authority and management responsibility of the user. Higher organizational altitude does not automatically mean greater operating detail.

Typical default relevance:

ASM: What requires action now?

SM / PM: What operating conditions require management intervention?

FOD: Which operating systems, managers, and constraints require attention?

GM: Which dealership conditions materially threaten the operating/economic result?

DWP: Where does leadership need to advise, direct, challenge, or intervene?

DWP does not use ProdTracker to remotely manage store operations. DWP provides the Digital FOD using MI V5 and uses higher-level intelligence to advise and direct leadership when appropriate.
