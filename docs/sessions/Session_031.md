## 1. Layer 3.5 Reasoning Orchestration — accepted

Reasoning Orchestration turns multiple individually valid Conditions and
diagnoses into the smallest coherent set of management priorities capable of
explaining and materially improving the operating state.

The following canonical rules were accepted:

### 3.5-R1 — Causal Compression

When multiple Conditions share a supported upstream cause, MI compresses them
into one causal family and one primary management priority. Correlation alone
does not authorize compression.

### 3.5-R2 — Localization Before Generalization

MI localizes a material failure to the lowest reliable operating level before
prescribing generalized corrective action.

### 3.5-R3 — Shared-vs-Independent Cause Validation

Conditions may be orchestrated into a common priority only when evidence
supports causal dependency, a shared cause, a shared intervention, or material
economic interaction. Otherwise, they remain independent priorities.

### 3.5-R4 — Existing-Intervention Awareness

MI considers active commitments and their expected observation horizons before
recommending a redundant or conflicting intervention.

### 3.5-R5 — Diagnostic Intervention

When causal confidence is insufficient for corrective action but materiality
warrants attention, MI may recommend a diagnostic intervention designed to
increase causal certainty.

### 3.5-R6 — Verification Before Correction

When evidence reliability is materially uncertain, verification takes
precedence over corrective intervention.

### 3.5-R7 — Intervention Conflict Resolution

MI does not present simultaneous recommendations whose execution materially
undermines a higher-value intervention without resolving the conflict by
sequencing, choosing, or modifying the actions.

### 3.5-R8 — Temporal Coherence

Conditions, diagnoses, interventions, and outcomes are interpreted relative to
their appropriate operating horizons. An intervention includes a start,
expected observation horizon, review point, and outcome evaluation.

### 3.5-R9 — Economic Interaction

MI evaluates dependency, overlap, and compounding effects before aggregating
economic opportunity.

### 3.5-R10 — Attention/Priority Separation

Attention classification describes management significance. Priority sequence
describes the preferred order of intervention. The two are related but are not
equivalent.

### 3.5-R11 — Role Coherence

Recommendations are translated to the lowest organizational level with the
authority, capability, and proximity to execute them. The Primary Action Owner,
Oversight Owner, and Escalation Owner may be different roles.

### 3.5-R12 — Causal-Recovery Validation

Observed outcomes following an intervention update confidence in both the
intervention and the causal hypothesis that produced it. Improvement after an
intervention does not, by itself, prove causation.

### 3.5-R13 — Material-Evidence Re-Orchestration

An established management priority persists until materially superseded,
resolved, invalidated, or displaced by a higher-order exception. Normal
variation updates evidence; material evidence may reorder priorities; a
Critical exception may reorder them immediately.

**Decision:** **3.5-R1 through 3.5-R13 ACCEPTED.**

**Layer 3.5 Reasoning Orchestration:** **ARCHITECTURALLY COMPLETE / FROZEN.**

## 2. Canonical orchestration flow

Layer 3.5 completes the following reasoning chain:

```text
Detect Conditions
    → Diagnose Causes
    → Establish Confidence, Materiality, Concern, and Urgency
    → Identify Shared vs. Independent Causes
    → Localize
    → Compress Causal Families
    → Recognize Existing Interventions
    → Resolve Intervention Conflicts
    → Account for Economic Interaction
    → Apply Temporal Context
    → Assign Role-Coherent Responsibility
    → Prioritize Management Attention
    → Produce the Management Position
    → Observe Outcomes
    → Validate or Revise Causal Belief
    → Re-orchestrate only on Material Evidence
```

The output is a Management Position containing the smallest useful set of
priorities. Each priority identifies why it matters, the supported causal
hypothesis, material evidence, True Potential gap, economic impact, confidence,
existing intervention status, recommended intervention, owner, timing, expected
outcome, and review horizon. Other valid findings remain visible as Monitor,
Existing Action, Diagnostic, Deferred, or No Action Required.

## 3. The “So What?” acceptance test

> **If MI merely restates something a competent manager could readily determine
> from the underlying reports, it has not produced sufficient Management
> Intelligence.**

MI output must materially improve at least one of:

- causal understanding;
- localization;
- prioritization;
- economic understanding;
- intervention selection;
- ownership;
- timing;
- confidence; or
- learning.

If it does not, MI should not consume management attention with the output.

## 5. Management Capacity safeguard

Management attention and execution capacity are finite operating resources.

> **MI shall limit active management priorities to the smallest set that can be
> responsibly executed within the available management capacity.**

Top 1–3 remains the normal operating objective, not an absolute cap. Independent
Critical Conditions may override normal compression. When the operation cannot
responsibly execute every valid recommendation at once, MI sequences, defers,
or monitors lower-value work rather than presenting an impossible action list.

This safeguard prevents technically correct intelligence from becoming
operationally unusable.

## 6. Minimum Viable Intelligence

**Minimum Viable Intelligence (MVI)** is the minimum trustworthy evidence from
which MI can begin producing useful management reasoning. MVI is not the
permanent limit of the system's intelligence and is not a reduced version of
the canonical architecture.

At MVI, a one-rooftop operator may provide ordinary dealership fundamentals
such as gross, volume, hours per RO, ELR, labor and parts margins, staffing,
scheduled hours, DPO, and basic history. From these, MI can identify economic
relationships, separate likely volume, rate, work-content, margin, and capacity
drivers, state uncertainty, and prescribe the next diagnostic or management
action.

Rich system data improves the depth and confidence of diagnosis, but it is not
a prerequisite for useful Management Intelligence.

---

## 7. The 4P evidence model

MI organizes available evidence across four business domains:

| Domain | Management question | Representative evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **People** | Do we have enough capable people, with clear accountability? | Staffing, roles, vacancies, capability, workload, ownership |
| **Operating Structure / Platform** | Is the operating system arranged to support the work? | Scheduled hours, DPO, capacity, teams, bays, systems, authority, coverage |
| **Process** | Are the right operating activities being executed correctly and consistently? | Inspection, presentation, authorization, production, follow-up, cadence |
| **Performance** | Are those conditions producing the intended operating and economic results? | Volume, Hrs/RO, ELR, margins, GP/RO, gross, forecast, Actual, True Potential |

The operator framing is:

> **The right number of people, in the right seats, doing the right things,
> producing the right results.**

The 4P model prevents MI from assuming every performance problem is a Process
problem. A weak result may instead originate in insufficient staffing,
misaligned roles, constrained capacity, broken structure, or another supported
cause.

## 8. Progressive Intelligence

MVI establishes the starting point. **Progressive Intelligence** describes how
MI becomes more capable through two independent but complementary paths:

1. **Evidence breadth:** additional People, Operating Structure/Platform,
   Process, and Performance evidence permits more precise localization and
   stronger causal discrimination.
2. **Longitudinal learning:** accumulated operating history, management actions,
   expected outcomes, observed outcomes, and changing Conditions allow MI to
   learn how this particular operation behaves over time.

Data richness and accumulated intelligence are not the same thing. MI can grow
more useful without a new source system by learning normal ranges, recurring
relationships, response to prior interventions, seasonality, and operating
patterns from the evidence already available.

Learned relationships remain probabilistic evidence. They are subject to
contradiction, decay, and revalidation as new evidence accumulates; they never
become permanent truth merely through repetition.

## 9. MVI validation tests

### Day-One independent dealer — pass

Using only ordinary operating and financial measures, MI separated a CP gross
gap into its economic components and identified declining work content per
visit as the dominant driver rather than volume or rate. A recent reduction in
advisor staffing supplied moderate evidence of a capacity relationship.

MI did not invent a Process diagnosis that the evidence could not support. It
identified what was known, what remained unknown, the economic materiality, the
causal confidence, and a diagnostic intervention to test the advisor-capacity
hypothesis.

**Result:** MVI produced useful Management Intelligence on Day One — **PASS**.

### Same dealer after six months — strong pass

With no richer source system, MI accumulated operating history, staffing
changes, interventions, expected outcomes, and observed outcomes. It learned
that advisor workload had previously approached a range associated with Hrs/RO
deterioration and CP GP/RO loss.

When workload later approached that range again—before lagging performance
failed—MI identified an emerging capacity risk and recommended review while the
outcome remained preventable. If the relationship failed to recur, MI was
required to weaken the learned belief rather than preserve it as doctrine.

**Result:** longitudinal learning advanced MI from reactive to anticipatory
reasoning — **STRONG PASS**.

## 10. True Potential Authority clarification

True Potential is a calculated representation of attainable operating
capability based on governed productive structure and basic economics. It is not
merely another target or comparison line.

> **When sufficiently complete, current, and validated, True Potential is the
> governing reference for determining attainable operating opportunity and
> supersedes weaker comparative references such as budget, prior-year
> performance, trend, or external benchmarks.**

The evidence hierarchy is:

- **Actual** describes what happened.
- **Validated True Potential** describes what the operation should reasonably be
  capable of producing.
- **Budget or Performance Objective** describes what management planned or
  committed to produce.
- **Prior year, trend, and benchmark** provide comparative context.

True Potential remains a testable governed model, not immutable truth. MI
continuously evaluates TP assumptions against observed operating capability and
sustained actual outcomes. It may flag TP as potentially **understated,
overstated, or stale**, identify the supporting evidence, and name the governed
assumptions requiring revalidation.

MI shall **not silently alter governed TP inputs**. Changes to technician count,
scheduled hours, DPO, productivity or efficiency assumptions, rates, margins,
available capacity, or other TP inputs must follow the applicable governance
and validation process.

The operating spine is:

> **MVI → True Potential → Actual vs. Potential → Condition → Diagnosis →
> Management Action → Outcome → Learning**

This clarification does not reopen frozen architecture. It makes explicit the
authority and quality requirements already inherent in the True Potential
model.
