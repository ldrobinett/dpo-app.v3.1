Status: FROZEN / PASS
Primary Objective: Define and smoke-test the canonical Management Intelligence™ architecture for workforce capability, coverage, dependency, development, leadership effectiveness, continuity, succession, intervention effectiveness, and MI self-evaluation while preserving human decision authority and minimizing management data-entry burden.

1. Session Outcome

Domain 8 is frozen.

Person → Role → Role Assignment → Capability → Coverage → Dependency → Development → Leadership Effectiveness → Continuity → Succession → Intervention Effectiveness → MI Self-Evaluation

MI evaluates operating evidence, capability, coverage, continuity, and intervention effectiveness. Management retains authority over hiring, termination, promotion, compensation, discipline, succession, and role assignment.

2. Canonical People Objects

Person

The individual associated with operating evidence, responsibilities, capabilities, assignments, and outcomes.

Role

A defined organizational responsibility with expected authority, accountability, operating scope, and capability requirements.

Role Assignment

The time-bounded relationship placing a Person into a Role for an organization, store, team, or operating scope.

Person, Role, and Role Assignment remain separate so MI can represent vacancies, temporary assignments, dual roles, interim coverage, cross-store coverage, promotions, transfers, and history.

The role exists even when the person occupying it changes.

3. Capability Intelligence

Capability: demonstrated ability to successfully perform work or responsibilities associated with a role.

Evidence priority:

demonstrated operating evidence;

repeated outcomes;

work/responsibility complexity;

independence and cross-situation evidence;

certifications/training;

experience/tenure;

manager classification/assessment.

Training, tenure, title, and résumé support an assessment but do not replace demonstrated current capability.

Capability Gap: material difference between capability required and demonstrated capability reasonably available.

MI may identify inconsistencies between formal classification and demonstrated evidence and request manager validation. MI assists classification; management retains authority.

4. Capability Coverage

Work Demand → Required Capability → Available Capability → Availability → Assignment → Coverage Gap → Operating Consequence

Coverage includes Quantity, Capability, Time, Location, Leadership, and Resilience Coverage.

Coverage Gap: a material difference between capability required and capability actually available within the relevant time, location, role, or work requirement.

Adequate headcount does not necessarily mean adequate capability coverage.

5. Workforce Dependency

MI reasons across Production, Capability, Discovery, Customer/Process Responsibility, and Leadership Dependency.

If this person becomes unavailable, what materially changes?

Dependency concentration is not automatically bad. MI intervenes when concentration creates material risk, constrains outcomes, or creates organizational fragility.

MI may recommend development, cross-training, staffing, assignment, or process changes to improve resilience.

6. Development Intelligence

What capability should be developed next, in whom, and why?

Development combines current capability, coverage gaps, dependency risk, work demand, demonstrated performance, and succession need.

A conceptual progression may include:

Learning → Assisted → Demonstrated → Independent → Advanced

Training is a means to capability, not the objective.

7. Leadership Effectiveness Intelligence

Is management consistently recognizing, acting on, and resolving the material conditions within its responsibility?

MI reasons through:

Condition Identified → Manager Awareness → Decision → Intervention → Timeliness → Operating Change → Persistence → Outcome

MI evaluates recognition, decision quality, execution, timeliness, effectiveness, persistence, and adaptation. It does not create a universal manager score.

Lagging results alone do not prove management quality.

8. Management Judgment vs MI Recommendation

MI recommendations are decision support, not commands.

If management rejects or modifies an MI recommendation and the alternative intervention proves effective, MI records the decision, evaluates the result, credits the effective intervention, reassesses its hypothesis, and learns.

Recommendation compliance is not leadership effectiveness.

9. Management Attention Demand

Management Attention Demand: an MI assessment of operating burden placed on a management role by current conditions, workforce structure, vacancies, dual-role assignments, team development needs, customer/operating risk, and unresolved constraints.

MI must distinguish weak follow-through from unreasonable management-capacity conditions.

10. Vacancy, Coverage & Continuity

MI understands Vacant Role, Interim Coverage, Cross-Store Coverage, Dual Role, New Assignment, and Leadership Transition.

Role changes become operating context for other domains.

Continuity Risk: operating risk created when a critical role, capability, responsibility, or body of operating knowledge lacks reasonable secondary coverage.

11. Succession Intelligence

Where does the operation have continuity risk, and who has demonstrated evidence of capability relevant to assuming greater responsibility?

MI does not decide who should be promoted.

For critical roles MI maintains a Role Capability Profile.

Succession readiness states:

Ready Coverage

Developing Coverage

No Demonstrated Coverage

Insufficient Evidence

Insufficient Evidence is not evidence of insufficient capability.

12. Role Readiness Review

For a person considered for a critical role, MI may show demonstrated evidence, limited evidence, capabilities not yet observed, evidence recency, developmental/temporary assignment evidence, management validation, and material selection risk.

MI assesses role-relevant capability independently from title, tenure, résumé, sponsorship, favoritism, nepotism, or management preference.

Management makes the employment decision.

13. Role Selection Risk

Role Selection Risk: evidence-supported operating risk created by assigning a person to a role when material required capabilities are unproven, insufficiently demonstrated, stale, or inconsistent with available evidence.

Possible classifications:

Low Evidence Risk

Moderate Evidence Risk

High Evidence Risk

Insufficient Evidence

This is decision support, not a hiring or promotion decision.

14. Assignment Validation

Assignment Validation: observing whether demonstrated, uncertain, and undeveloped role capabilities are validated through actual operating performance after a role assignment.

New or temporary assignments may create focused validation around known evidence gaps without recurring competency paperwork.

15. Opportunity to Demonstrate

MI must distinguish absence of demonstrated evidence from evidence of absent capability.

Unequal opportunities to demonstrate leadership, technical, operational, or management capability must be considered.

MI may identify a Succession Evidence Gap and recommend controlled developmental assignments.

16. External Candidates / New Hires

Prior experience, résumé, certifications, references, and prior titles are supporting evidence.

Until current operating capability is demonstrated:

MI Demonstrated Capability: Insufficient Evidence

After assignment, MI accumulates current evidence through Assignment Validation.

17. Continuous Succession Intelligence

MI continuously accumulates:

Role Assignments → Demonstrated Capability → Developmental Assignments → Interventions → Outcomes → Temporary Coverage → Evidence Recency → Management Validation

Monthly Succession Evaluation

MI performs and preserves a formal monthly succession evaluation for critical roles, including continuity status, validated/developing coverage, candidates requiring validation, capability gaps, new/stale evidence, and changes in risk.

On-Demand Succession Review

Authorized leaders may trigger an immediate current evaluation when a role becomes vacant or circumstances materially change.

An on-demand review creates a new dated evaluation event and does not overwrite monthly history.

Quarterly Organizational Review

At higher altitude, MI summarizes bench strength, continuity risk, development progress, and validated coverage without unnecessary personnel detail.

18. Organizational Bench Strength

MI may summarize:

Critical Roles → Ready Coverage → Developing Coverage → No Demonstrated Coverage → Insufficient Evidence

The purpose is to identify organizational fragility and development needs, not rank people.

19. Intervention Effectiveness Intelligence

Condition → MI Diagnosis → Recommended Action → Management Decision → Action Taken → Expected Effect → Observed Effect → Learning

MI separately evaluates:

Management Intervention Effectiveness: Did the management action produce the expected operating change?

MI Recommendation Effectiveness: Did the MI-recommended action produce the predicted targeted change and intended downstream outcome?

20. MI Self-Evaluation

Self-Evaluation Principle

MI must evaluate the effectiveness of its own recommendations by tracking execution, predicted operating change, persistence, and intended downstream outcome, and use those results as evidence to improve future reasoning.

Attribution Discipline

MI must not claim its recommendation caused an outcome merely because the outcome improved afterward. It distinguishes recommendation, management action, observed change, and supported causal confidence.

Failed interventions trigger reassessment, not mechanical repetition.

21. MI Effectiveness Intelligence

MI evaluates the health of the:

Store ↔ Management ↔ MI operating loop

Potential dimensions include Signal Quality, Recommendation Quality, Management Adoption, Intervention Effectiveness, Outcome Effectiveness, Persistence, Self-Correction, and Evidence Quality.

MI Effectiveness Score

At DWP/enterprise altitude, MI may provide a store-level composite score for triage and program oversight, accompanied by trend and the primary constraint when intervention is needed, such as Management Adoption, MI Recommendation Quality, Evidence Quality, or Structural Constraint.

The MI Effectiveness Score is not an employee performance score.

22. Quarterly MI Effectiveness Review

A structured quarterly tenant/store review validates recommendation quality, management adoption, intervention effectiveness, sustained improvement, evidence quality, realized value, and areas where MI itself needs improvement.

MI should provide evidence that the management system is helping, not merely evidence that the software is being used.

23. People Evidence Acquisition

MI acquires workforce intelligence in this order:

Automatically Observed — normal operating evidence.

Connected Systems — roster, roles, hierarchy, schedules, availability, training, certifications, vacancies, employment status, effective dates.

MI Inference — capability, coverage, dependency, development, leadership effectiveness, attention demand, continuity, succession, selection risk.

Human Validation — judgment, intent, confirmation, exception, role interest, succession validation, classification, and context MI cannot reliably observe.

Observe → Integrate → Infer → Ask

24. Minimum Manager Input Principle

MI should never request information from management that can be reliably observed, calculated, integrated, or reasonably inferred from existing evidence.

Human input is reserved for:

Judgment → Intent → Confirmation → Exception → Context

25. People Intelligence Guardrails

Operational Evidence Only — MI evaluates job-relevant evidence, not character, motivation, intelligence, loyalty, personality, or personal worth.

Evidence Is Not the Person — weak evidence in one capability is not a global judgment.

Opportunity to Demonstrate — lack of evidence does not equal lack of capability.

Management Authority — people decisions remain human decisions.

Evidence Independence — MI does not rewrite evidence merely to agree with management.

Explainability — material people conclusions are traceable to understandable evidence.

Recency — capability evidence ages; recent repeated evidence generally deserves greater weight.

No Forced Ranking — MI does not rank employees merely because it can.

26. Management Authority / Evidence Independence Principle

Management retains authority over hiring, promotion, succession and role assignment. MI retains independence in its assessment of available operational evidence.

MI learns when subsequent outcomes show management judgment was stronger than MI's recommendation, and when MI's evidence-based assessment proves useful.

Strong Financial Results / Weak Leading Conditions: MI identifies rising dependency/execution/continuity risk despite good lagging results. PASS

Overloaded Manager: MI identifies leadership capacity/attention constraint instead of simplistic follow-through failure. PASS

Wrong Succession Inference: Management rejection is recorded; candidate is not repeatedly resurfaced absent material new evidence. PASS

MI Gives Bad Advice: Manager's alternative works; MI credits it, marks original hypothesis unsupported, and learns. PASS

Key Technician Dependency: Advanced WIP concentration traced to capability dependency rather than manager blame. PASS

31. Session Close

Traditional workforce systems ask:

Who works here, what is their title, and did they complete training?

Domain 8 enables MI to ask:

What capability does the operation require, what capability is demonstrated, where are coverage and dependency risks, who is developing, what leadership actions are working, where is continuity fragile, what succession evidence exists, and was MI itself right about the action it recommended?

The goal is not to automate human judgment.

The goal is to give human judgment better evidence.
