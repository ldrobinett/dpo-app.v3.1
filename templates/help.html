ProdTracker Help Guide

Quick-start guide for managers and department leaders

1. What ProdTracker Does

ProdTracker helps dealership managers understand whether the department is on pace, where the production risks are, and what action should happen next.

ProdTracker is built to drive the behavior required to hit daily targets and monthly forecast. What gets measured gets managed.

Pace to Forecast

Compares actual performance to the monthly financial forecast and shows whether the shop is ahead or behind.

Daily Production Focus

Shows today’s target, today’s load, appointment pressure, and workflow bottlenecks that require manager attention.

Tech & Workflow Visibility

Highlights which technicians are behind pace and which repair orders are behind promise time.

2. Daily Manager Workflow

The system works best when managers use it in the same order each day.

Recommended Daily Order
  1. Input Daily Metrics
    Enter previous workday gross and appointment data. This takes less than one minute.
  2. Update the Route Sheet
    Confirm the shop workflow matches what is happening in the DMS. If your store uses the route sheet throughout the day, advisors and technicians can use it to track workflow and log work hours, giving the department real-time workflow visibility.
  3. Reconcile Work Logs
    Confirm hours logged in ProdTracker match actual flagged technician hours.
  4. Review the Home Page
    Check pace, bottlenecks, late work, and technician pace.
  5. Use a Calculator if Needed
    Open the recommended tool from Today’s Focus when deeper diagnosis is needed.
Important: most stores report previous workday gross and hours, not live same-day financial performance. The homepage is built around that reality.

3. Understanding the Home Page

Today’s Focus

This is the fastest summary of what needs attention right now. It identifies the primary issue, explains the impact, suggests actions, and recommends a tool or route for deeper review.

Daily Targets

This card compares your monthly forecast to your projected month-end result and shows how much daily recovery is needed. It includes:

  • forecasted gross
  • projected month-end gross
  • monthly FRH goal
  • projected month-end FRH
  • base daily target
  • updated daily target including recovery need
Appointment Information

Shows whether the store has enough appointment load to support the day and near-term horizon. This helps answer: Do we have enough traffic to support production?

Today’s Work Opportunity

Estimates how much current workflow is available to support today’s target. This is based on route sheet status mix and weighted WIP logic. It is directional, not final accounting.

Hours Pace

Shows expected MTD FRH versus actual MTD FRH and how much work is still needed to stay on plan.

Route Sheet Action Board

Combines workflow status counts, bottleneck alerts, and the oldest overdue repair orders into one action area.

Bottom 5 Techs / Top Performers

Shows which technicians are furthest behind pace and which are leading the month.

4. Setup Requirements

ProdTracker is only as strong as the setup behind it. These items must be maintained first.

Initial Setup

Use the Manage area to upload team data and build the initial schedule. The tool provides a CSV template that can be completed and uploaded.

Team Members

Every technician should have the correct team, tech number, and daily production objective. During initial setup, use Team Members in Manage to either manually set or calculate daily production objectives. Calculated objectives are preferred.

Schedules

Team schedules and PTO/training entries drive staffing assumptions and pace calculations. Schedules should be reviewed and updated at the beginning of each month so forecast and calculator outputs reflect actual technician availability.

Financial Inputs

ELR, parts ratio, margins, unapplied time, and other financial values affect forecast and calculator outputs. These values should be reviewed and updated at the beginning of each month to keep the monthly forecast accurate.

Monthly Forecast

The homepage pace logic compares current results to the monthly forecast. If the forecast is wrong, pace guidance will be wrong.

5. Beginning of Month Manager Checklist

At the beginning of each month, managers should confirm the department is set up correctly before relying on pace, forecast, or calculator outputs. If monthly setup is wrong, homepage guidance and calculator results will be wrong.

Beginning of Month Priorities
  1. Confirm technician schedules are accurate
    Make sure technician schedules, PTO, training days, and other availability changes are entered so staffing assumptions and forecast logic reflect reality.
  2. Update monthly financial numbers
    Confirm forecast, ELR, labor margin, parts ratio, parts margin, unapplied cost, and any other financial assumptions are current for the month.
  3. Review the monthly forecast
    Make sure the store’s forecast reflects current month expectations. The homepage pace logic depends on this being right.
  4. Use calculators to identify improvement opportunities
    Managers should use the calculators to understand what needs to improve, including appointments, RO count, gross per RO, technician capacity, and pace recovery.
Important: before using calculator outputs to guide action, confirm schedules and monthly financial inputs are updated for the current month.

6. Route Sheet

The Route Sheet is the workflow board for open repair orders. Managers should use it to see where work is stuck, whether enough jobs are loaded, and which customers are at promise-time risk.

  • Dispatch = work available but not moving yet
  • Inspection = diagnostic / inspection phase
  • Approval = waiting on approval
  • Parts = delayed by parts
  • Service = actively being worked
  • Ready / Warranty = effectively closeable or near closeable work
Route Sheet bottlenecks usually require workflow action, not calculator action.

7. Work Log & Reconciliation

The Work Log stores logged FRH by technician and repair order each day. Reconciliation compares downloaded DMS data to ProdTracker entries so your pace and technician reporting stay accurate.

Why it matters
  • hours pace depends on logged FRH
  • real-time production data is generated from technician work logs
  • this data feeds your production displays so technicians can stay engaged throughout the day with their DPO
Use Manage > Reconcile Logs after downloading hours from the DMS. Easy access is also provided from the homepage by clicking the Tech Hours Sync badge.

8. Financial Inputs & Forecast

The financial dashboard is where the monthly forecast begins. The home page uses those values to determine whether the department is on pace and how much recovery is needed.

Key financial sources
  • Monthly Forecast: Financial Dashboard / Forecast tools
  • MTD Actual Gross: Daily Metrics input
  • MTD Actual FRH: Work Log / Reconciled hours

9. Calculators

Calculators are for diagnosis and what-if planning. Use them when Today’s Focus points you there, and also at the beginning of the month to identify what should improve in appointments, RO count, gross per RO, technician capacity, and recovery pace.

Calculator Best Used For
CP Menu / MPI Opportunity Calculator Estimating gross impact from tenths, MPI opportunity, and CP menu improvements.
RO & Appointment Calculator Estimating daily RO need, appointment need, and capacity support.
Gross Profit Opportunity Calculator Comparing current gross profit per RO to an opportunity state.
Capacity Opportunity Calculator Comparing current shop capacity and financial output to an improved state.
Pace Recovery Calculator Understanding how much daily recovery is needed to close the monthly gap.
Appointment Lift Calculator Estimating how much extra gross and FRH added appointments can generate.

10. Common Definitions

FRH

Flat rate hours. This is the production language used throughout the site.

DPO

Daily production objective for a technician.

ELR

Effective labor rate used in financial modeling and calculators.

WIP

Work in process. Open work currently in the workflow.

CP

Customer pay work.

WP

Warranty pay work.

11. Troubleshooting

Check the four most common causes first:
  • daily metrics were not entered
  • route sheet is not updated
  • work logs were not reconciled
  • monthly forecast or financial inputs are outdated

Sunday is treated as a readiness view. The homepage is designed to help managers prepare for Monday, not measure live Sunday production.

Start with these items in order:
  1. daily metrics input
  2. route sheet update
  3. work log reconciliation
  4. monthly forecast and financial inputs
  5. team member objectives and schedules
If you can't figure it out: contact support@dealerwarrantyprocessing.com.
